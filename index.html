Sovereign Compute for AI Safety

AI labs around the world, including China, want to safety-test their models. However, these labs are too compute starved to allocate sufficient resources for safety. Pacific Compute operates a Singapore cluster that can only be used for AI safety.

The problem

AI labs allocate compute based on a hierarchy of needs.

Chinese labs want safety testing but can’t spare the chips.

Skip past this scrolling figure

In order to continue to operate, an AI lab must spend its compute resources from the bottom up. The first layer is inference, which is the layer that generates revenue. It serves the model that customers pay for.

Next comes compute for training and capability research. If a lab stops training the next better model, it quickly falls behind.

Inference, training, and capability research are the three tiers that keep a lab in business.

Only then can a lab spare compute for safety. For example, misuse safety tests to protect against potential harm via biological, cyber, or other attacks.

At the top is compute for alignment, to make sure the model itself behaves as intended.

Chinese labs do not have enough compute for safety. Every Chinese company together owns about 5% of the world’s leading AI compute, less than any single top US hyperscaler. In contrast, the five US hyperscalers own over 70%. US and allied export controlsU.S. and allied rules that restrict sales of advanced AI chips and chipmaking tools to China. on AI chips hold them back.

What little compute Chinese AI labs do have goes to the bottom of the pyramid. At Qwen, Alibaba’s model team, “just fulfilling delivery requirements already consumes the vast majority of our compute”. Safety is neglected.

This neglect isn’t for lack of concern. In January 2026, Z.ai warned investors of “grave harm, even catastrophe”. Yet only five of ten leading Chinese developers reported safety evaluation results with any release. DeepSeek, Moonshot AI and MiniMax published almost no safety research from January 2025 to October 2026. Smaller labs may be unable to afford safety teams. What looks like recklessness can usually be explained by a shortage of chips.

The safety of Chinese AI models matters beyond just China. The world now runs on these models. On OpenRouter, a gateway companies use to reach many AI models, Chinese models’ share of tokens rose from 6–13% in February to 57–67% by mid-September 2026. Cursor, the US coding tool, built its Composer 2 model on Moonshot’s Kimi K2.5. Qwen alone has more than 113,000 derivative models, each built on its weights. Two leading Chinese models perform about as well on cyber tests as closed models released 4 to 7 months earlier, and open weightsA model whose trained parameters are published for anyone to download and run. cannot be recalled.

The solution

Pacific Compute closes the gap in compute for AI safety.

Skip past this scrolling figure

Pacific Compute supplies the top of the compute pyramid: a not-for-profit cluster of NVIDIA H200s in Singapore, reserved for safety work including dangerous-capability evaluationsTesting whether a model can materially help someone cause serious harm, for example in biology, cyberattacks or autonomous tasks., red-teamingDeliberately attacking a model to find ways it can be made to misbehave., and interpretabilityResearch into what is happening inside a model, by studying its internal workings.. We are funded by private philanthropy and our first cluster is live.

Pre-release evaluations of frontier open models are free, and the unreleased model stays on the lab’s servers while our GPUs run the tests around it. Our cluster’s compute is only used for AI safety purposes.

Findings privately go to the lab before release, while there is still time to act and update the model. The lab can choose whether or not to publish the results.

Singapore is a place that labs from China and the U.S. can both use. Our rules restrict the cluster to safety work only: no capability work, no gradient updatesA training step that changes a model’s weights. by default, and no political work.

Conclusion

AI Safety requires its own compute.

Chinese labs desire to test their models for safety, but their compute runs out before they are able to. Pacific Compute provides safety-only GPUs in Singapore, so open models can be analyzed for safety before they’re shipped.
